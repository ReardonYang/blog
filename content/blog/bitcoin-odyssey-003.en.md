* **Satoshi doesn't mind being misread, just as Confucius didn't. Confucius wasn't afraid of being misread either** — he had three thousand disciples, seventy-two of them accomplished. Are we to believe there wasn't a single rotter among those seventy-two, or that every one of them understood and carried out Confucius's thought one hundred percent?

* Even Craig Wright and Jihan Wu are spreading Satoshi's ideas according to their own understanding of the white paper. Buddhism has a saying: "If the Dharma is to flourish, monks must praise monks." Why must monks praise monks? In *Journey to the West*, when Ananda and Kasyapa hand over the scriptures, they set conditions and openly shake down Monkey for a protection fee — and it's clearly not their first time. Yet they sit secure in Thunderclap Monastery§. What religion in history hasn't seen blood flow like rivers, inside and out?

* **A powerful idea doesn't fear being misunderstood, doesn't fear being deliberately distorted. It fears only that it cannot spread, and that there is no soil for it to spread in.**

* Bitcoin, and the 12,801 cryptocurrencies derived from it (as of 02:42, 18 October 2021), will spread through this world like a virus. For a virus, what matters is not whether the direction of mutation is the one it wanted — what matters is whether there are new hosts to infect.

* Confucius died, but he left a trillion people as his stand-ins: Ethereum, SHIB, TRON… If any one of them manages to usurp Bitcoin's throne, I will welcome it at any time — that would mean Bitcoin has completed its mission. A stand-in being made official is the most normal thing in the world.

* These 12,801 assorted cat-coins, dog-coins and pig-coins, and the countless rising stars yet unborn, are all Bitcoin's stand-ins. **If Bitcoin dies one day, then it deserved to die, and I will say: good riddance!**
